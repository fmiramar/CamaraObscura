- Computer class: iMacPro1,1, six-core Intel Core i5 reported at 4.1 GHz,
  16 GB RAM.
- OS/kernel: Darwin 24.6.0, x86_64.
- Compiler: Apple Clang 17.0.0.
- Build: CMake `Release`, C++17, native x86_64 executable.
- SuperCollider installed version: 3.14.1.
- Sample precision: `float`.
- Nominal conversion rate: 48,000 samples/second.
- One run per kernel after process startup; results are not claimed as a

“Counted state” is the benchmark’s explicit delay, modal, spectrum, FIFO, and
scratch storage. It excludes executable code, graph objects, allocator
overhead, model Buffers, and other server state.

## Interpretation

The FDN core scales close to linearly and is inexpensive at the supported
32-line maximum. Modal rendering is also linear but intentionally much more
expensive: 2,048 modes consumed about 30.5% of one measured core, so 4,096
modes are supported for design headroom rather than promised as cheap on every
machine.

The 4096-sample partitioned convolver is computationally efficient even at
eight outputs, but its spectrum storage dominates memory and it imposes
4096 samples of latency. Six partitions represent up to 24,576 source-tail
samples; a longer Dark Velvet profile increases spectrum memory and FFT
multiply work in direct proportion to partition count.

These numbers explain kernel cost, not audible quality. Echo density, energy
decay, spectral flatness, channel correlation, and geometry accuracy are
tested separately because no single reverb score captures them.
